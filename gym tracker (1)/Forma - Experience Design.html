Assets: an honest constraint you need to decide on

I cannot produce photoreal, Blender-authored gym equipment from inside this environment, and I can't license or download third-party GLB models into your project. That is a real limit and it directly affects the quality bar you set, so I'm raising it before building rather than after.

What I can build to a genuinely premium standard is procedurally authored, high-fidelity three.js geometry — lathed and extruded compound forms with chamfered edges, knurled grips, real bolt and joint detail, correct PBR materials, HDRI environment lighting, ACES tone mapping and contact shadows. That is a very long way from "primitive boxes and cylinders", and on a black studio background with the right lighting it reads as convincing hardware. It is not the same as a scanned or modelled real product.

My recommendation: commission or license four hero models (adjustable dumbbell, barbell + plate set, bench, functional trainer) and let me author everything else procedurally to match. Section 07 lists every asset, which tier it belongs to, and its poly and file budget. Tell me which route you want and I'll build to it.

01 · Creative direction

One object. One camera. One continuous transformation. The website is not a page with 3D on it — it is a single mechanism the visitor operates by scrolling.

The thesis

Forma is the operating system between a coach, their clients, their programming, their communication and their business. A page of feature cards cannot say that. A machine that evolves can. So the entire site is built on one idea, stated mechanically:

TRAINING EQUIPMENT → TRAINING SYSTEM → COACHING SYSTEM → FORMA

As a coach's practice gets more advanced, the system grows with them. We show that by never introducing a second object. There is exactly one object graph in the scene for the entire scroll, and it is rebuilt — dumbbell into barbell into bench into rack into functional trainer into the Forma dashboard. Nothing ever cross-fades out and something else in. Every part has a destiny (Section 06).

Five rules the build cannot break

  1. Continuity over spectacle. If the visitor can't tell that the thing they're looking at used to be the dumbbell, the transformation has failed. Persistent part identity is the whole trick.
  2. Scroll is the only clock. No autoplay loops competing with the user. Every camera move, rotation, separation, morph, light change and UI reveal is a function of scroll progress. The one exception: idle micro-motion in the environment lighting, so a paused scene isn't dead.
  3. The software must look like real software. The environment is cinematic; the interface inside it is not. No holographic HUDs, no sci-fi glyph borders, no floating translucent panels. The Forma dashboard rendered in 3D space should look like something a coach could click.
  4. Copy is a caption, not an argument. Two lines per beat, maximum. The animation carries the explanation. Where the brief supplied copy, I use it verbatim.
  5. Orange is a pointer, not a paint. Accent appears on collars, pins, light strips, active indicators, edge ignition and the F mark. Equipment stays steel, rubber and charcoal.

What this replaces

No hero-plus-feature-grid. No icon triads. No testimonial carousel. No screenshot-in-a-browser-frame. No decorative objects orbiting a headline. The current site's information still gets delivered — it's just delivered by the mechanism instead of by cards.

CH 03 · Assembly · 280vh · pinned

The system evolves

Sees
The longest, most mechanical passage on the site. A bench arrives and assembles under the bar. Uprights rise. A rack forms around it. Then the rack itself reconfigures into a full functional trainer with pulleys, cables and weight stacks. Continuous, weighted, deliberate.
3D
Three movements. i — Bench: frame tubes slide in on the floor plane, feet rotate down and plant, the pad and its bolts settle last. ii — Rack: uprights telescope up from the bench frame's rear tubes; J-hooks index into position; the bar lifts and seats into them; copper adjustment pins snap home. iii — Trainer: uprights extend further and split into twin towers, pulley housings mount and their sheaves start turning, cables thread through and take up tension with real catenary sag, weight stacks build plate by plate from the same plates that started as dumbbell plates.
Camera
A single continuous crane. Low and close on the bench bolts, rising and pulling back as the uprights extend, arcing 70° around the right tower as the pulleys mount, settling wide and slightly high as the stacks complete. One move, no cuts. Focal length drifts 35mm→28mm to let the structure feel large.
Learns
This is the thesis in mechanics: the same system, evolving as the coaching gets more advanced. Nothing was replaced.
Copy
Sparse and late. Two mono captions during the movements — MORE CLIENTS. / MORE PROGRAMMING. / MORE TO RUN. — then, on the completed machine: The system grows with you.
Transition
The machine is complete and still. The camera begins moving into it, toward the gap between the towers. As it enters, edges nearest camera take a thin sienna ignition line.
CH 04 · Materialization · 220vh · pinned · THE HINGE

Metal becomes interface

Sees

Which assets need real GLB, and how to get them

Tier A is five models. Options, in order of my preference:

  1. Commission (recommended). Five models, hard-surface, PBR, shared material library, delivered as Draco-compressed GLB with a consistent scale and origin convention. This is the only route that reliably clears your quality bar and guarantees the five assets share a visual language — which the transformations depend on.
  2. License. Marketplace gym-equipment models exist at usable quality, but they come from different authors, which means mismatched topology, scale, material conventions and detail density. Anything licensed needs a retopology and material-unification pass before it can transform believably.
  3. Procedural stand-ins from me now, real models swapped in later. Fastest path to a reviewable experience. The architecture in Section 14 keeps asset loading behind a single provider, so swapping a procedural part for a GLB is a one-file change per asset and does not touch any animation code.

Shared visual language is a hard requirement, not a preference. All equipment must use one material library — same steel, same rubber, same powder-coat, same accent anodizing — and one scale convention. If the dumbbell's steel doesn't match the trainer's steel, T2 through T5 stop reading as one evolving system and the site's entire argument collapses.

Rules

  • Any UI the visitor must read sits within 18° of orthogonal to camera. Beyond that it becomes texture, not information.
  • Type inside the 3D world uses the same scale and weights as the real product. No condensing, no letter-spacing tricks, no glow.
  • No translucent panels, no scan lines, no corner brackets, no animated borders, no monospace-everything. Panels are #111111 with a rgba(230,226,220,.15) hairline — the design system's own dark-card treatment.
  • The accent appears in UI exactly where a real product would use it: active nav item, primary button, progress fill, one highlighted metric. Never as ambient glow.
  • Depth is communicated by position and parallax, not by blur on the thing you're meant to read.

09 · Desktop composition

A fixed compositional contract across all fourteen chapters, so the visitor's eye never has to re-learn where to look:

11 · Performance strategy

This has to remain a production website that ranks and converts, not a demo.

Budgets

  • Initial critical payload (to first meaningful frame): ≤2.5MB — fonts, HDRI, hero dumbbell, app shell.
  • Total 3D payload across the full scroll: ≤4MB, Draco for geometry, Meshopt for the instanced sets, KTX2/BasisU for every texture.
  • Frame budget: 16.6ms desktop, 22ms tablet. Draw calls ≤180 at peak (Ch03 and Ch12 are the peaks).
  • Lights: two shadow-casting maximum, ever. One 1024px shadow map, plus a baked contact-shadow plane.

Techniques

  • Adaptive DPR — measured frame time drives device pixel ratio between 1 and 2, with hysteresis so it doesn't oscillate visibly.
  • Render on demand — frameloop="demand" outside active scrubbing; invalidate on scroll and on the idle-motion tick only.
  • Hard stop when off-screen — an IntersectionObserver on the canvas wrapper pauses rendering entirely for Ch15 and anything above the fold on return visits. Also pauses on visibilitychange.
  • Instancing — plates, bolts, library cards and hierarchy nodes are all InstancedMesh. Ch12's hierarchy is one draw call.
  • Frustum and LOD — Tier A models ship at two LODs; the low LOD is used whenever the part is beyond mid-depth, which is most of Ch03 and all of Ch12.
  • Post-processing — SMAA always; a tightly-thresholded bloom that only the accent emissive can trigger; a subtle vignette. DOF only in Ch04, 07 and 08, and disabled below 1280px.
  • Code splitting — each chapter's scene module is a dynamic import, prefetched one chapter ahead.
  • prefers-reduced-motion — the whole film degrades to fourteen static hero frames (one authored keyframe per chapter) with the full copy deck as normal scrolling DOM. No parallax, no scrub, no autoplay. This is a first-class path, not an afterthought.

SEO and accessibility layer

The complete narrative — every headline, every supporting line, every feature list from Chapters 05–12 — exists as real, semantic, crawlable DOM: one h1, sequential h2s per chapter, real lists, real links. The canvas is aria-hidden="true" and purely presentational. Copy is positioned over the canvas in normal document flow rather than injected by the animation system, so it is present at first paint, indexable, translatable, screen-reader-navigable, and survives with JavaScript disabled via <noscript>. Keyboard users get chapter-to-chapter navigation through the progress-rail ticks as real focusable buttons.

Lighting rig — one rig, all fourteen chapters

  • Environment: a single neutral studio HDRI at 0.7 intensity, providing all reflection and ambient. It is rotated slowly by scroll — this alone keeps every surface alive without any animated light.
  • Key: a large rect area light, high and camera-left, 3.2 intensity, warm-neutral 5200K. The only shadow caster for the physical chapters.
  • Rim: a narrow rect light behind and camera-right at 1.8, cooled slightly, to separate silhouette from the black void. This is what makes the equipment read as premium.
  • Fill: a very low hemisphere at 0.25 so shadow interiors aren't pure black.
  • Contact: a baked radial-gradient shadow plane beneath the object, scaled to its footprint. Cheaper and softer than a real shadow map for ground contact.
  • Practical accents: from Ch04 onward, emissive accent geometry is the primary light source — the interface lights its own scene. Key and rim drop to 30% across the Ch04 hinge, which is what sells "this is no longer a lit physical object."
  • Grade: ACES filmic tone mapping, exposure 1.05, output sRGB. Warm shadow lift of about 2% so black is never dead — matching the design system's warm-tinted shadow convention.

15 · Animation architecture

One master timeline. Scroll is the only input. No chapter runs its own independent clock.

scroll → Lenis (smoothed) → ScrollTrigger (pin + scrub:1)
       → globalProgress 0..1
       → per-chapter t 0..1  (normalized, clamped, overlap-aware)
       → keyframe resolvers
            camera(t)      → position · target · fov · roll
            parts(t)       → transform · material · visibility  per part
            ui(t)          → mount · opacity · depth · layout state
            lighting(t)    → intensities · env rotation · exposure
            post(t)        → bloom threshold · dof focus
       → single render (invalidate)

Rules

  • Scenes are data. Each chapter is a plain manifest — { id, length, pin, camera[], parts{}, ui{}, copy{} } — declared in one file. Pacing and choreography are editable without touching component code, which is what makes art direction iterable.
  • Overlap windows. Adjacent chapters share a 12% crossfade window on camera and lighting tracks only, so the film has no seams. Part transforms never overlap — they hand off at exact boundaries so mechanics stay legible.
  • Everything is interpolation. No .to() chains firing on triggers, no autoplay tweens, no timeline that can desync from scroll position. Scrub backwards and every frame is identical to the forward pass — a hard requirement, and the thing most scroll sites get wrong.
  • Easing belongs to the mechanism. Mechanical actions (T1–T5) use a firm settle with a slight overshoot on seat and snap. Digital actions (T6–T20) use the design system's cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1). Metal lands; interface eases.
  • Idle life. When scroll is still, the only motion permitted is HDRI rotation drift and a ≤2mm object float. Enough that the frame breathes; not enough to compete with the user.

16 · Asset-loading strategy

Four waves, each gated so the visitor never scrolls into an empty scene, and never waits at a spinner mid-film.

WaveContentsGate
0Fonts (Poppins, DM Mono), HDRI, hero dumbbell, app shell, all copyHard gate. The Ch00 preloader. Nothing renders until resolved.
1Barbell, plate set, bench, Ch02 fragment texturesFetched during Ch01. Soft gate at the Ch01→02 boundary.
2Rack, functional trainer, pulleys, stack, cable mapsFetched during Ch02. Soft gate at the Ch02→03 boundary — the heaviest wave, and it has a full chapter of runway.
3Phone, product UI textures, exercise media, food photographyFetched during Ch03/04. Soft gate before Ch07.

A soft gate means: if the wave hasn't resolved when the visitor reaches the boundary, scrub decelerates and holds at the chapter's final frame with a 1px copper progress tick at the rail — the film pauses on a composed image rather than showing a loader. In practice, with a full chapter of runway per wave, this should almost never trigger.

All Tier A geometry is Draco-compressed and loaded through a single shared GLTFLoader with a Meshopt decoder; all textures are KTX2/BasisU with mip chains. Frame sequences for mobile are fetched in ranges of 12 frames with two ranges of lookahead in the scroll direction. Everything is served immutable with long-lived cache headers and content-hashed filenames.
